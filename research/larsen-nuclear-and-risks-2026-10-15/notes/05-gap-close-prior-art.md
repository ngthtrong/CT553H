## GAP 1 — The *Cell Systems* 2026 "interpretable biophysical neural network" on tile libraries: identity, granularity, splits, noise ceiling, and whether it pre-empts the residue-level degron claim

### Takeaway

The paper is real and correctly described in outline: **LeBlanc et al., "Interpretable biophysical neural networks of transcriptional activation domains separate roles of protein abundance and coactivator binding," *Cell Systems* 22 Sept 2026 issue (vol. 17, no. 9, art. 101701), DOI 10.1016/j.cels.2026.101701** — it does separate abundance from function on a tile library, it does use a **protein-level/homology-clustered split**, and it does **benchmark against a replicate-reproducibility ceiling**. But it is an **activation-domain** paper, not a degron paper: it never trains or evaluates on Larsen or Voutsinos degron tiles (it cites them only as supporting literature), its predictions are **per-tile scalars**, and it contains **no residue-level prediction and no residue-level evaluation** of any kind. **Verdict: it does not pre-empt the claim of "first residue-supervised and residue-evaluated degron model," but it does destroy the novelty of three supporting framings** (abundance-vs-function separation on tile libraries, protein-clustered splitting of tile libraries, and comparing to a measured replicate ceiling), which must therefore be re-worded as "standard practice, following LeBlanc et al. 2026" rather than as thesis contributions.

**Architecture, input, output granularity** — [FULL-TEXT]

- Shared building block: "one-hot encoded activation domain sequence … applies a **convolutional filter**, followed by a **Leaky ReLU**, a **fully connected (linear) layer with a single output**, and a final Leaky ReLU." Single convolutional filter only — "multiple convolutional filters rapidly became uninterpretable." Filter size and random seed were swept; "the random seed … had a larger impact on performance than the convolutional filter size." — [PMC13596073, Results, "The data and neural network architecture"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- **Input**: a single 40-aa tile, one-hot encoded. **Output**: one scalar per tile — GFP (AD strength), mCherry (abundance), or both jointly. — [PMC13596073, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- Parameter count: **138 parameters for the ShallowNN**, vs 96,769 for their own ADhunter residual NN and 8 × 80,005 for the ensembled PADDLE — i.e. 700× and ~4,500× fewer. — [PMC13596073, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- **The "biophysical parameters" are per-tile equilibrium constants, not per-residue energies.** "The BiophysicalNNs predict protein abundance and the equilibrium constants using the basic building blocks … The predicted equilibrium constant(s) are used to calculate the amount of TF in the bound state. Finally, a function is applied to predict the total amount of GFP expression from the amount of bound TF. We found that a **hill function** leads to the highest performance." Two models: a **two-state** binding model (unbound ⇄ coactivator-bound) and a **three-state** "acidic exposure" model (collapsed ⇄ expanded ⇄ bound). Three assumptions: strength ∝ amount in bound state; abundance = sum over all states; system at equilibrium. — [PMC13596073, Results, "Incorporating Biophysical Models into Neural Network Architecture"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- The only position-resolved object is a **model-parameter matrix**, not a prediction: "These matrices show the effect of each residue at each position in the filter, similar to the matrices from deep mutational scans … In our ShallowNN, the matrices have **horizontal streaks, indicating that composition is more important than individual positions** in these intrinsically disordered activation domains." The convolutional filter is interpreted as composition weights, the linear layer as positional weights (N→C within the 40-mer). — [PMC13596073, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- The filter weights are **not stable per position**: "Individual positions in each filter are highly variable, but the overall sum of the weights applied to each amino acid is similar, consistent with the large role of composition and weak grammar. The convolutional filters are **not picking up on specific protein motifs** and instead are picking up composition patterns." (assessed over 30 random seeds × 200 bootstraps) — [PMC13596073, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)

**Residue-level predictions / residue-level evaluation: NO** — [FULL-TEXT]

- Exhaustive grep of the parsed full text for `per-residue`, `residue-level`, `single-residue`, `saturation`, `variant effect`, `mutagenesis` returned **zero** matches in the body text. Every reported metric is a **per-tile** Pearson/Spearman correlation or a tile-level PR/ROC curve on n = 2,521 test sequences. — [FULL-TEXT, parsed PMC13596073]
- Where a residue-level competitor existed, they **collapsed it to tile level**: "ADPred takes any input size but **produces a predicted value for each residue** instead of a single score for the entire sequence. We ran TADA and ADPred on the 40aa tiles in the test set. **To get a single value for ADPred, we averaged the predictions for the 40aa tile.**" — [PMC13596073, STAR Methods, "Comparison with other NNs"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)

This led to **over one million mutated tiles** … we calculated the change in activation domain strength between the wildtype and mutant tiles." No residue-level labels and no residue-level metric are involved. — [PMC13596073, STAR Methods, "Deep NN interpretations"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)

**Split: protein-level / homology-clustered — YES** — [FULL-TEXT]

- "Because of the tiling process, many sequences in our dataset were closely related, with some differing by as few as five amino acids. This overlap means that **if our NN memorized the training data, it could still perform well on the test dataset**, depending on how the sequences were split … We **hierarchically clustered the full-length ortholog sequences**, split the sequences into three clusters, and then assigned tiles to each cluster based on the ortholog sequence from which they originated. In this way, **tiles from the same full-length sequence would not be in the training, validation and test split**. Additionally, we added all control sequences to the test set. As a final test, we verified that there was **minimal correlation between the similarity of sequences in the test set to the training set and the prediction accuracy**." — [PMC13596073, STAR Methods, "Dataset preparation"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- The same splitting procedure was reapplied to the PADI *Arabidopsis* dataset, and the deep baseline ADhunterLite "is trained using the **same train/validation/test splits** as our ShallowNNs and BiophysicalNNs." — [PMC13596073, STAR Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- Permutation testing for parameter significance was also split-aware: "we shuffled data labels together (strength and abundance) within each data set (train/validation/test) to **preserve biases within our data splits**," with the seed fixed to the best model's seed to capture seed bias. — [PMC13596073, Quantification and Statistical Analyses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)

**Measurement-noise ceiling / replicate reproducibility — YES, explicitly** — [FULL-TEXT]

- "The abundance data was **much noisier than the GFP data (replicate Pearson correlation of 0.568)**, and our best performing NN **approached this ceiling (Pearson correlation of 0.555**, p-value < 10⁻³⁰⁰) on unseen test data." — [PMC13596073, Results, "The shallow neural network for predicting transcription factor abundance"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- Replicates are used to build the labels themselves: tiles present in both replicates were averaged into a single score. — [PMC13596073, STAR Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- A second, orthogonal noise axis is quantified: **random-seed variance dominates architecture choice.** "The random seed generally impacted NN weight variability more than the training data" (30 seeds × 200 bootstraps), and "most convolutional filter sizes fell within the noise caused by random seed." Framing: "all NN variations performed similarly, with individual differences likely coming from differences in random seeds rather than the inherent benefits of one architecture over the other." — [PMC13596073, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- Accuracy benchmarks for context (per-tile, test set n = 2,521): ShallowNN GFP Pearson **0.837** vs one-hot linear regression 0.801; ShallowNN slightly below ADhunter-GFP and PADDLE on both regression and classification; two-state and three-state BiophysicalNNs "performed comparably to the ShallowNN," and both "generalized slightly better than the ShallowNN on new data." — [PMC13596073, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)

**What the paper claims as its own novelty (so the thesis can avoid colliding with it)** — [FULL-TEXT]

- "Previously, the impact of TF abundance in high-throughput activation domain screens had either been **ignored**, been **normalized through ratio sorting**, or **controlled for with gating**. Here, we took an alternative approach and used BiophysicalNNs to **model the relationship between abundance and activation domain strength directly**, which allows us to **deconvolve bifunctional roles of amino acids**." — [PMC13596073, Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- Stated goal is *not* accuracy: "Our goal was not to outperform the existing predictors, but merely to increase interpretability while achieving similar accuracy." — [PMC13596073, Introduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)
- Two biological findings: C-terminal hydrophobic residues simultaneously increase AD strength and decrease TF abundance; acidic residues at the N-terminus promote coactivator binding while acidic residues at the C-terminus promote TF abundance. — [PMC13596073, Abstract + Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13596073/)

### Inferences

- **[INFERENCE]** The claim "first residue-supervised and residue-evaluated degron model under a protein-cluster split against a measured label ceiling" survives this paper on its decisive clauses. LeBlanc et al. is (a) not about degrons, (b) not residue-supervised, (c) not residue-evaluated. Their only position-resolved artefact is a 20 × *k* weight matrix *inside* a 138-parameter model, which they themselves interpret as evidence that **composition beats position** in their data. Nothing in it is scored against residue-level labels.
- **[INFERENCE]** However, three sub-claims that a thesis might have been leaning on are now clearly *not* novel and should be demoted to "following established practice": (1) **separating abundance from function on a tile library** is the headline of this paper; (2) **protein-level/homology-clustered splitting of overlapping tiles**, with an explicit anti-memorisation rationale *and* a test-to-train-similarity-vs-accuracy control, is done here on tiles that overlap by 35/40 residues — a stricter leakage setting than 28-mer degron tiles; (3) **benchmarking a model against a replicate-reproducibility ceiling** is done numerically here (0.555 vs 0.568). The defensible residue of the thesis novelty is therefore narrow and must be stated precisely: *residue-level supervision and residue-level evaluation, on degron/stability data, against a residue-level measured ceiling.*
- **[INFERENCE]** Their "composition > position, weak grammar, filters do not detect motifs" result is a **substantive scientific risk** to any thesis that assumes a residue-level signal is recoverable from tile-level degron labels. They swept 30 seeds × 200 bootstraps on 17,732 tiles and found per-position filter weights "highly variable" while per-amino-acid sums were stable. If the same holds for degron tiles, a residue-level model may be fitting positional noise. The thesis should pre-empt this by showing per-position stability across seeds/bootstraps — an experiment this paper supplies the template for.
- **[INFERENCE]** A reviewer who knows this paper will ask why the thesis does not use a biophysical head. The honest differentiator is the *label*: LeBlanc et al. had **two paired measurements per tile** (mCherry and GFP) which is what makes the two-state/three-state identification possible. A degron tile library with a single stability readout cannot identify those equilibrium constants, so the biophysical-NN route is not directly available — a defensible methodological reason, not a gap.
- **[INFERENCE]** Their noise-ceiling figure also sets an expectation bar: a replicate Pearson of 0.568 on abundance in a 40-aa Sort-seq library suggests degron-tile replicate agreement may be in a similar band, and that reporting *model r* without *replicate r* will be read as a reporting failure in 2026.

Figure panels themselves were not viewed — only the figure legends (which are complete and were parsed). ## GAP 2 — The *PLOS Computational Biology* 2026 "edge-aware GAT": does solvent accessibility gate the pooling step? (and the same question for BoM-Pooling)

### Takeaway

The paper is real: **Yang, Zhang, Qiu, Xiao & Lin, "Edge-aware GAT-based protein binding sites prediction," *PLOS Comput Biol* 22(8):e1014552, 11 Aug 2026**, fully open access. The decisive answer is **no**: relative solvent accessibility (RSA) enters **only as an atom-level input node feature** (alongside 8-class DSSP), the **edge features are purely geometric** (Euclidean distance + unit direction vector), and the attention pooling is a **content-based pooling driven by a learnable query vector — it is not gated, weighted or biased by accessibility**. BoM-Pooling (Hoang & Singh, *Bioinformatics* 2025) is **sequence-only and contains no structural or accessibility signal at all**. So the thesis novelty of "an exposure (rASA/pLDDT) gate at the pooling/aggregation step of an MIL architecture rather than at the input" is **not pre-empted by either paper** — with one real caveat (below) that a reviewer can and probably will raise.

### Cited Findings

**Exact identity of the edge-aware GAT paper** — [FULL-TEXT] (JATS metadata from Europe PMC)

- Title: *Edge-aware GAT-based protein binding sites prediction*. Authors: **Weisen Yang, Hanqing Zhang, Wangren Qiu, Xuan Xiao, Weizhong Lin** (corresponding; ORCID 0000-0002-7537-1899). Editor: Lun Hu. — [PMC13460574](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)
- Journal *PLOS Computational Biology*, volume **22**, issue **8**, e-location **e1014552**, pub-date **11 Aug 2026**. DOI **10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014552**; PMID **42579672**; PMCID **PMC13460574**; manuscript id PCOMPBIOL-D-26-00007. Open access (`isOpenAccess: Y`). — [doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014552](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014552)

**Task, inputs and the dataset** — [FULL-TEXT]

- Task: **residue-level binary binding-site classification across five interaction classes** (protein–protein, DNA/RNA, ion, ligand, lipid), built on atom-level graphs. "we propose an Edge-aware Graph Attention Network (Edge-aware GAT) model for the fine-grained prediction of binding sites across various biomolecules." — [PMC13460574, Abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)
- It is explicitly a **re-engineering of PeSTo**: "we replaced PeSTo's global interaction modeling with sparse, edge-aware graph attention mechanism." Inputs: "Atomic coordinates, element types, and structural descriptors (DSSP, RSA) from PDB files," with a 5.0 Å Euclidean neighbourhood cutoff and 4 GAT layers, 32-dim hidden space. — [PMC13460574, Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)
- Split: **PeSTo-style clustered benchmark at 30% max sequence identity between clusters**, ~70/15/15 → 376,216 / 101,700 / 97,424 chains. Importantly, the authors disclaim stronger homology control: "**we did not construct an additional independent homology-controlled benchmark in this study.** Future work will further evaluate the model using stricter sequence-identity-based splits, such as CD-HIT- or MMseqs2-based partitioning." — [PMC13460574, Discussion + Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)
- Headline result: ROC-AUC **0.93**, vs PeSTo 0.91, EGNN 0.88, AtomGNN 0.86, ScanNet 0.87, MaSIF-site 0.80, Sppider 0.73, PSIVER 0.64. Protein–protein accuracy 0.89→0.93, F1 0.69→0.77 over PeSTo; lipid F1 0.21→0.32, MCC 0.20→0.46. — [PMC13460574, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)

**WHERE accessibility enters: input node feature only** — [FULL-TEXT] — *this is the decisive evidence*

- "The atomic-level features of the model comprise five components: **element type, residue category, atom type, secondary structure and relative solvent accessibility (RSA)**. The first three features … were formulated as described in [PeSTo] … Additionally, we incorporated two features: secondary structure information and RSA. The secondary structure was classified using an **8-category scheme derived from DSSP** … **RSA values were normalized to range [0,1] to represent the degree of residue exposure** on the protein surface." Missing DSSP/RSA values are mean-filled from homologous residues. — [PMC13460574, Methods, "Feature extraction"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)
- The architecture figure legend states the same split of roles unambiguously: "**Node (atom-level) features** include element type, atom type, residue category, DSSP-derived secondary structure, and **relative solvent accessibility (RSA)**. **Edge features** comprise pairwise Euclidean distances and unit direction vectors between connected atoms." — [PMC13460574, Fig. "Overview of the Edge-aware GAT method"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)
- Edge/attention term is **geometry only, no accessibility**: the attention coefficient is computed from "the transformed source-node feature, target-node feature, and **edge embedding**," where the edge embedding is "**Euclidean distance … and unit direction vector**." "the Euclidean distance and the unit direction vector are encoded as geometric edge attributes and used to **modulate the attention coefficient** between connected atoms." The authors explicitly decline equivariance: "this module should be interpreted as a **geometry-aware and direction-informed attention mechanism rather than a rotation-equivariant tensor propagation module**." — [PMC13460574, Methods, "Model architecture"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)

**The pooling step: learnable query vector, NOT an accessibility gate** — [FULL-TEXT]

- Section heading is literally "**Residue-level pooling and decoder**": "An **attention-based pooling aggregates atomic features to the residue level. Given a pooling center vector**, the attention weight for residue *i* is computed as …" — i.e. the pooling weights come from a learned query/centre vector applied to learned node states. — [PMC13460574, Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)
- Stated purpose of the pooling design, in the authors' own words: "we incorporated a **learnable query vector** into the attention-based residue pooling mechanism to **adaptively focus feature aggregation on functionally relevant regions**. This approach enhances state consolidation." — no mention of exposure, burial, rASA or pLDDT as the gating quantity. — [PMC13460574, Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)
- Ablations treat RSA and pooling as **two separate, independent components** — further confirming RSA is not the pooling signal: "the exclusion of secondary structure or Relative Solvent Accessibility (RSA) descriptors led to **ROC-AUC reductions of 3% and 4%** respectively … **replacing attention-based pooling with average pooling decreased the MCC from 0.83 to 0.71**, while reverting to single-scale neighborhood modeling reduced the lipid-binding F1-score by 5%." Removing edge features entirely dropped F1 0.89→0.77. — [PMC13460574, Methods, "Ablation experiments"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)
- The authors' own three-component summary names geometry at the edges and attention (not exposure) at the pooling: "by integrating **multidimensional structural features, geometry-aware edge attention, and residue-level attention pooling** …" — [PMC13460574, Conclusion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)
- No post-hoc accessibility filter either; the only post-processing is a fixed 0.5 probability threshold and atom→residue aggregation: "we aggregated atoms with predicted binding probabilities above a threshold of 0.5 into residue-level binding sites." — [PMC13460574, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460574/)
- `pLDDT` does not appear anywhere in the paper; the inputs are experimental PDB structures, not AlphaFold models. — [FULL-TEXT, exhaustive grep of parsed PMC13460574]

**BoM-Pooling (Bioinformatics 2025): sequence-only, no accessibility anywhere** — [FULL-TEXT]

- Identity: **Minh Hoang & Mona Singh (Princeton, Lewis-Sigler Institute / CS), "Locality-aware pooling enhances protein language model performance across varied applications," *Bioinformatics* 41(Suppl 1), 15 Jul 2025**, DOI **10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf178**, PMID 40662798, PMCID PMC12261463 (ISMB supplement). — [PMC12261463](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12261463/)
- Mechanism: "Our method first **cascades the sequence into smaller overlapping *k*-mers and performs local Avg-Pooling within each window**. This set of pooled *k*-mers is subsequently condensed into a fixed-sized vector using the above **attention pooling** mechanism." Variants: **self-attention pooling** (single-sequence regression/classification) and **cross-attention pooling** (pairwise/interaction tasks). Motivation is purely computational — full attention pooling is quadratic in length, BoM-Pooling is quadratic only in the number of *k*-mers, giving a 5–12× training speedup. — [PMC12261463, §3.3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12261463/)
- The pooling weights are learned from **PLM embeddings alone**; the gate quantity is learned relevance, not a physical covariate: "incorporating a **learnable attention mechanism within the pooling layer** to adaptively focus on relevant regions … and assign higher importance to features or residues that are crucial to understanding its function." — [PMC12261463, Introduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12261463/)
- **No structural input of any kind.** An exhaustive grep of the parsed full text for `solvent`, `accessib`, `rASA`, `RSA`, `pLDDT`, `exposure`, `buried` returned a **single incidental hit** (the word "structure" in "hierarchical classification of protein structures," describing the SCOPe label source for the remote-homology task) — no accessibility feature, no exposure weighting, no structural gate. Tasks are FLUO (fluorescent-protein fitness), BLAC (β-lactamase activity), SCOPe remote homology (~20,000 proteins, 150–1,022 aa), and phosphotyrosine signalling domain–peptide interactions. — [FULL-TEXT, parsed PMC12261463]
- Performance claim, for context: up to **11.4% median improvement** over parameter-matched existing pooling techniques; wins 15 of 17 experiments across 4 tasks × up to 5 PLMs. — [PMC12261463, Abstract + Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12261463/)

### Inferences

- **[INFERENCE]** The thesis novelty survives, but the precise wording matters. What exists in the literature as of Oct 2026 is: (a) **accessibility as an input feature** (Yang et al. 2026, and standard practice before it), and (b) **learned, content-based attention pooling** (Yang et al. 2026 atom→residue; Hoang & Singh 2025 residue→sequence). What does **not** exist in either paper is a pooling/aggregation weight that is an **explicit function of a physical exposure covariate (rASA or pLDDT)**. So the claim should be phrased as "an *explicit, non-learned (or exposure-conditioned) gate* on the MIL aggregation weights," not merely "accessibility at the pooling step."
- **[INFERENCE]** **The reviewer objection to prepare for**: in Yang et al., RSA is an input node feature that is propagated through 4 GAT layers and then consumed by the attention-pooling query. A learned attention pooling over nodes whose features already include RSA **can in principle express an exposure gate** — it is a strict superset of the thesis's hand-placed gate. The thesis therefore cannot rest on "nobody put exposure near the pooling step"; it has to rest on an **ablation** showing that the explicit gate beats a learned attention pooling *given the same RSA/pLDDT input features*. Yang et al.'s own ablation table (attention pooling vs average pooling: MCC 0.83→0.71; −RSA: ROC-AUC −4%) is the exact comparison template, and notably they never ran the cross-term (learned pooling *without* RSA inputs vs RSA-conditioned pooling), which is the empty cell the thesis can occupy.
- **[INFERENCE]** Both papers are weak on homology control relative to what the thesis is claiming. Yang et al. explicitly state they did not build a homology-controlled benchmark beyond the inherited PeSTo 30%-identity clustering and flag CD-HIT/MMseqs2 splits as *future work*; Hoang & Singh use SCOPe-derived splits only for the homology task and had to exclude TM-Vec from comparison because its CATH training data contains test-set homolog pairs. A thesis that ships an MMseqs2 protein-cluster split is therefore ahead of both on evaluation hygiene — a defensible secondary contribution.
- **[INFERENCE]** Neither paper is a multiple-instance-learning architecture in the formal sense (bag of instances → bag label with no instance labels). Yang et al. have **per-atom labels available** and pool atoms to residues for *output formatting*, not to solve a weak-supervision problem; Hoang & Singh pool residues to a sequence embedding for a sequence-level label but never frame it as MIL or evaluate instance-level attributions. The MIL framing of the thesis is thus genuinely distinct from both, and should be stated as such.

### Gaps

- The task brief described the PLOS paper as using "attention-based pooling" and "rASA as an input feature" in "protein residue/site prediction" — that matches exactly. But it is a **binding-site** paper on experimental PDB structures, **not a degron/stability paper and not an AlphaFold/pLDDT paper**, so its relevance to the thesis is purely architectural prior art, not domain prior art. Worth stating plainly in the thesis's related-work table.
- I did not exhaustively search for *other* papers that place an accessibility gate at the pooling step (e.g. in a non-GAT MIL setting, or in peptide/epitope MIL). The negative result here covers the two papers named in the brief only; a broader "no one has done this" claim would need a dedicated systematic search and should be labelled accordingly (see the repo convention on evidence labels).
- Supplementary tables of both papers (full ablation grids, hyperparameter sweeps) were not retrieved; the numbers above are from main-text prose and figure legends.

## Bonus check — *Nature Chemical Biology* review on "degrons beyond short linear motifs": is a 28-mer window a scope risk?

### Takeaway

The review exists and is correctly identified: **Baiyun Wang & Ning Zheng, "Degrons and degradation signals beyond short linear motifs," *Nature Chemical Biology* 22(3):355–368**, published online **14 Nov 2025**, print March 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41589-025-02056-2. It is **paywalled and I could not read the full text** — findings below are **[ABSTRACT-ONLY]**. On the question asked: **yes, the abstract does argue that degrons are substantially not reducible to short linear motifs**, on two distinct grounds — degrons mapped to **high-order protein structures** (hence "cryptic"), and **nonproteinaceous signals** that are not in the amino-acid sequence at all. This is a genuine, citable scope risk to a 28-mer-window thesis and should be addressed head-on in the limitations section.

### Cited Findings

- Identity and venue — [ABSTRACT-ONLY / metadata]: Wang B, Zheng N. *Nature Chemical Biology* **22**(3):**355–368**. ### Inferences

- **[INFERENCE]** The risk is real but **bounded and partly orthogonal** to a 28-mer thesis. Decompose the review's two arguments:
  1. **"High-order structure" degrons** — these are still *sequence-resident*; a 28-mer window can contain them, but the window cannot represent the 3D context that makes them active or cryptic. This argument therefore attacks *sequence-only* 28-mer models, and is **answered**, not refuted, by conditioning on structure (rASA/pLDDT) — which is precisely what the exposure-gate design does. The review is thus better cited as **motivation** for the structural gate than as a threat.
  2. **"Nonproteinaceous signals"** (cofactors/ligands/PTM-adjacent cues residing in the substrate, the E3, or their interface) — these are **genuinely outside any peptide window**, of any length, and outside the tiling assay's reach. This is an irreducible ceiling on 28-mer tile data and should be stated as an explicit out-of-scope boundary, not argued against.
- **[INFERENCE]** Crucially, the Voutsinos et al. assay itself already carries the same scope assumption and reports the same structural caveat — "Structural mapping shows that **many degrons are buried and likely become active upon protein unfolding**" ([Science Advances abstract, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adz3483](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adz3483), [ABSTRACT-ONLY]). So the 28-mer window scope limitation is **inherited from the label source**, not introduced by the thesis. The correct framing is: the thesis models *what the 30-residue tiling assay can measure*, and the Wang & Zheng review defines what lies beyond that measurement — a limitation shared with the primary data, which is a much easier position to defend at a viva than claiming the window is sufficient.
- **[INFERENCE]** The two reviews point in mildly opposite directions (Wang & Zheng: motifs are insufficient and degrons are architecturally diverse; Zhang et al.: high-throughput screens are rapidly expanding high-resolution degron definitions). Citing **both** is the honest move and defuses the scope objection better than citing either alone.

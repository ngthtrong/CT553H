## Q1. Label-ceiling / noise-ceiling evaluation: how published work reports performance relative to the reproducibility ceiling, and how psychometric reliability thinking transfers

### Takeaway

There is no single canonical "noise ceiling" protocol in the protein-ML literature, but there are three citable patterns: (i) report the replicate/independent-assay correlation as an explicit upper bound next to the model's score (ProteinGym appendix; Livesey & Marsh's cross-assay correlations; RECOVER's "highest achievable Spearman"); (ii) import the neuroscience noise-ceiling recipe (split-half reliability → Spearman–Brown correction → square root = ceiling on correlation); (iii) import classical test theory's attenuation correction. For this thesis the strongest and most defensible move is (i) + (ii), reported as a ratio ("fraction of ceiling attained"), **not** a disattenuated point estimate presented as the headline number.

### Cited Findings

**Protein/variant-effect literature that states the ceiling explicitly**

- **[DID]** ProteinGym's appendix on measurement noise states plainly: *"Experiments do not have a perfect dynamic range, often imposing a restrictive ceiling and/or floor"*, *"noise is a perennial issue in high-throughput assays, and some assays have poor experimental replicate correlation"*, and *"Taken together, this means that one cannot expect perfect correlation between experiment and model."* — [ProteinGym (bioRxiv full text), Appendix A.2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.07.570727v1.full)
- **[DID]** ProteinGym chooses Spearman as the primary metric because of *"the often non-linear relationship between protein function and organism fitness"*, concluding *"the Spearman's rank correlation coefficient is the most generally appropriate metric"* (§4.1) — [ProteinGym](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.07.570727v1.full)
- **[DID]** ProteinGym supplements Spearman with AUC and MCC because *"in situations where DMS measurements exhibit a bimodal profile, rank correlations may not be the optimal choice"*, plus NDCG (which *"up-weights a model if it gives its highest scores to sequences with the highest DMS value"*) and **Top-K recall with K = top 10% of DMS values** (§4.1) — [ProteinGym](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.07.570727v1.full)
- **[UNVERIFIED — search summary only]** A DMS benchmarking paper (Livesey & Marsh) treats correlations between **independent DMS datasets of the same protein** as a rough guide to how well a perfect predictor could score; reported range **0.94 (bla a/b) down to 0.34 (PTEN a/b), average 0.66** across pairs, with the caveat that *incremental* condition changes gave high correlations while changes of assay type / binding partner gave much lower ones — so a single per-protein ceiling can mislead. Primary text not retrievable (EMBO/PMC blocked). — [Livesey & Marsh, Mol Syst Biol](https://link.springer.com/article/10.15252/msb.20199380); [PMC copy of the updated benchmarking paper](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10407742/)
- **[DID]** A direct precedent for reporting a *noise-aware upper bound alongside the achieved score*: the RECOVER drug-synergy paper reports **"RECOVER achieves 0.47 Spearman correlation, while the highest achievable Spearman correlation is estimated to be 0.64"**, and motivates it with *"given that the observations are noisy, the observed rank among synergies might be corrupted compared to the true ordering"* — [RECOVER, arXiv:2202.04202](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.04202)
- **[DID]** ProteinGym's own uncertainty reporting for model-vs-model differences is *"the non-parametric bootstrap standard error of the difference"* between a model's Spearman and the best model's (Tables A5/A14 captions) — [ProteinGym](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.07.570727v1.full)
- **[DID]** Protein-stability precedent for "the labels are themselves model-derived, hence noisy": ThermoMPNN was trained on Megascale ΔΔG° values *inferred from protease-sensitivity experiments*, which the authors benchmark against earlier direct measurements at **Pearson 0.75–0.96** — i.e. the dataset's own agreement with independent measurement is reported as context for model scores — [ThermoMPNN (bioRxiv full text)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.07.27.550881v1.full) / [PNAS version](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.2314853121)
- **[UNVERIFIED — third-party GitHub analysis, not peer-reviewed]** A worked example on the Tsuboyama 2023 dataset reports **replicate SD ≈ 0.075 kcal/mol** as an empirical noise floor, and that **20.3% of single substitutions have no ddG_ML value at all** (dynamic-range censoring skewed toward the most destabilising variants) — [megascale-measly](https://github.com/tremgan/megascale-measly)

**The neuroscience / psychometrics machinery and how it transfers**

- **[PRACTICE]** The neuroscience noise-ceiling idea: the ceiling is *"estimated from the best-possible model"* and an upper bound can be *"constructed by computing the RDM which performs best among all possible RDMs"* — [Statistical inference on representational geometries, arXiv:2112.09200](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.09200)
- **[DID]** A concrete split-half recipe worth copying verbatim: *"We therefore estimated a noise ceiling from the Spearman-Brown-corrected split-half reliability of the mean ratings. The human noise ceiling was then taken as the square root of this reliability."* — [arXiv:2604.07254](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07254)
- **[PRACTICE]** Attenuation correction (Spearman's disattenuation): `r_true = r_xy / sqrt(ρ_xx · ρ_yy)`. Documented with the warning that **underestimated reliabilities can push the corrected value above 1**, so the corrected value should be reported with a confidence interval — [Attenuation correction calculator / docs](https://metricgate.com/docs/attenuation-correction-disattenuation/); [Correction for attenuation explainer](https://www.cogn-iq.org/learn/theory/correction-for-attenuation/); [The Correction for Attenuation Due to Measurement Error: Clarifying Concepts and Creating Confidence Sets](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7746510_The_Correction_for_Attenuation_Due_to_Measurement_Error_Clarifying_Concepts_and_Creating_Confidence_Sets)
- **[PRACTICE]** A *partial* attenuation correction exists for when you distrust your reliability estimate, with an application to sample-size calculation — useful because a full correction at ρ≈0.24 is numerically violent — [Modifying Spearman's Attenuation Equation to Yield Partial Corrections for Measurement Error, PMC5965627](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5965627/)
- **[PRACTICE]** Spearman–Brown is a *different* tool from disattenuation: it predicts reliability at a different test length (here: number of replicates averaged), not a corrected correlation — [The Spearman-Brown Formula and Reliabilities of Random Test Forms, arXiv:2308.13811](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13811)
- **[DID]** A biology precedent for applying Spearman's correction to real measurement noise: an mRNA–protein correlation study notes *"measurement noise attenuates estimates of correlations between mRNA and protein level"* and that *"if the noise and the signal are independent, this decomposition and the Spearman's correction are exact"* — [Post-transcriptional regulation across human tissues, arXiv:1506.00219](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.00219)
- **[PRACTICE]** ICC is the standard reliability coefficient for test–retest designs and has *"been specifically developed for this purpose"*; conventional thresholds cited are ICC > 0.7 ("good") and > 0.75 ("good to excellent") — [Beyond the intraclass correlation: a hierarchical modeling approach to test-retest assessment (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.04.425305v2); [Intraclass correlation: improved modeling approaches and applications for neuroimaging (bioRxiv)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/164327.full.pdf)
- **[DID]** Important nuance for this thesis: the same authors argue reliability *"tends to be underestimated under the conventional intraclass correlation framework"* because the conventional ICC conflates trial-level noise with between-subject reliability, and propose a hierarchical model that separates them — [Beyond the ICC](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.04.425305v2)
- **[PRACTICE]** Aggregating across measurements raises reliability: a task-fMRI study found reliability was only "moderate" per task but *"aggregating fMRI BOLD signal changes across the tasks improved test-retest reliability metrics"* — [search-surfaced via the ICC topic pages](https://www.science.gov/topicpages/t/test-retest+intraclass+correlation) **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **[PRACTICE]** The general framing that measurement error is a first-order driver of irreproducible findings (useful one-line citation for a thesis introduction) — [Loken & Gelman, "Measurement error and the replication crisis", Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aal3618)

### Inferences

- **[REC] The single most defensible evaluation table for this thesis** puts the replicate itself in the results table as a competing "predictor", i.e. rows: `rep1 → rep2` (the ceiling), `model → rep1`, `model → rep2`, `model → consensus/mean`, plus a shuffled-label and a composition-only baseline. This mirrors (not copies) Livesey & Marsh's cross-dataset comparison and RECOVER's "highest achievable Spearman", and it needs no contested statistical correction — a committee can read it directly.
- **[REC] Report a ratio, not a corrected correlation, as the headline.** `fraction of ceiling = metric(model, y) / metric(rep1, rep2)`. Keep disattenuation (`r/sqrt(ρ)`) as a secondary, clearly-labelled sensitivity analysis with a bootstrap CI, because at ρ ≈ 0.24 the divisor is ≈ 0.49 and any error in ρ is amplified — exactly the failure mode the attenuation literature warns about (corrected values exceeding 1).
- **[REC] Arithmetic worked for this thesis's audited numbers** (my computation from the given figures, not from any paper — the thesis must cite its own audit file for the inputs). Writing `y = t + e` with reliability `ρ = Var(t)/Var(y)`, a perfect predictor of `t` correlates with a single observed replicate at `sqrt(ρ)`:
  - residual-variance noise fraction 0.759 ⇒ single-replicate reliability `ρ₁ ≈ 0.241` ⇒ **max attainable Pearson vs one replicate ≈ 0.491**;
  - Spearman–Brown for the mean of k = 2 replicates: `ρ₂ = kρ₁/(1+(k−1)ρ₁) = 2(0.241)/1.241 ≈ 0.388` ⇒ **max attainable Pearson vs the 2-replicate mean ≈ 0.623**;
  - taking the reported "learnable-context ceiling ≈ 0.087 of variance" as `R²` against a single replicate ⇒ **context-only ceiling r ≈ 0.295 vs one replicate**, which implies `corr(model, t) ≤ 0.601`, and therefore **≈ 0.374 (R² ≈ 0.140) against the 2-replicate mean**.
  - **Practical consequence:** simply training and evaluating against the **mean of both replicates instead of one** lifts the attainable Pearson by roughly 27% relative (0.295 → 0.374) with zero modelling effort. This is the cheapest single win available and should be decided before any architecture work.
- **[REC]** ICC(2,1) or ICC(A,1) (two-way random, absolute agreement) on the per-peptide ΔPSI across the two replicates is the right reliability coefficient to report for the *continuous* target; Cohen's κ (or Jaccard, which the audit already has) for the *binary* position target. Report both plus their bootstrap CIs — the ICC gives you the `ρ` for every formula above.

### Gaps

- I could not open Livesey & Marsh or the updated-benchmarking PMC article (EMBO redirect to Springer; PMC behind a browser check). The 0.34/0.94/0.66 numbers and the exact framing of the "ceiling" are therefore **unverified** and must be read from the PDF before citing.
- I found **no protein-ML or MAVE paper that reports a formally disattenuated metric as its headline number.** The uncertainty-quantification benchmark for protein engineering reports plain RMSE/MAE/R²/Spearman with no disattenuation step ([PLOS Comput Biol](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1012639)), and a 2026 peptide–protein affinity benchmark likewise uses raw Spearman ([arXiv:2608.30175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30175)). So "disattenuated metrics" would be a **novel presentation choice** in this field, not an established one — argue it, don't assume it.
- No source found that gives a closed-form noise ceiling for **set-overlap metrics (Jaccard/F1 on position sets)** analogous to `sqrt(ρ)` for correlations. The empirical `rep1 vs rep2` Jaccard is the only defensible ceiling I can source.

## Q2. Soft / probabilistic / distributional labels: variance-aware training, heteroscedastic regression, precision weighting, errors-in-variables, censored likelihoods

### Takeaway

The MAVE field's own analysis pipelines already solve "label + per-label variance" properly and are the right precedents to cite: Enrich2 (weighted least squares / random-effects across replicates), DiMSum (explicit additive + multiplicative error model, then an error-weighted average across replicates), and Rosace (hierarchical shrinkage that pools variance information *across positions*). On the ML side, Kendall & Gal's heteroscedastic Gaussian NLL is the standard "predict mean and variance" loss. The cheapest and best-precedented option for this thesis is **precision-weighted regression on the replicate mean**, with heteroscedastic NLL as the stretch goal.

### Cited Findings

**MAVE/DMS error models (the field's own precedent for per-example variance)**

- **[UNVERIFIED — search summary; Genome Biology paywalled]** Enrich2 estimates per-variant scores by regression on counts across time/bins and reports that *"replicate agreement for scores calculated using the weighted least squares regression model is higher than agreement for scores calculated using ordinary least squares regression"* — i.e. precision weighting measurably improves reproducibility of the labels themselves — [Enrich2 / Rubin et al., Genome Biology 2017](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13059-017-1272-5); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28784151)
- **[DID — from the search-returned description of the paper and its pipeline docs]** DiMSum's error model decomposes variance into **count-dependent (multiplicative, one term per input sample and per output sample, per replicate) and count-independent (additive) parts**, fitted by minimising the gap between observed and predicted variance across variants, and fitted *over every subset of ≥2 replicates* to pin down the additive terms. Fitness is `log(output/input)` relative to wild type, and *"replicate-specific errors then drive a weighted average across replicates."* The authors state DMS data are over-dispersed relative to Poisson and that the model *"accurately captures deviations of the empirical variance from Poisson expectation"*; they argue per-variant error estimates are *"underpowered with the 3 to 6 replicates typical of DMS work"*, so variance information must be **shared across variants** — [DiMSum (bioRxiv full text)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.06.25.171421v1.full); [Genome Biology version](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13059-020-02091-3); [pipeline docs, stage 5.3](https://github.com/lehner-lab/DiMSum/blob/master/docs/PIPELINE.md)
- **[UNVERIFIED — title/abstract only, Springer paywalled]** Rosace is *"a robust deep mutational scanning analysis framework employing position and mean-variance shrinkage"* — i.e. a Bayesian hierarchical model that borrows strength **across residue positions** and shrinks variance estimates. This is the closest published analogue to the thesis's problem (position-level labels, few replicates) — [Rosace, Genome Biology 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-024-03279-7)
- **[DID]** popDMS is another published inference framework for extracting mutation effects from DMS read counts — alternative to Enrich2 if the thesis needs a second method for sensitivity analysis — [popDMS, Bioinformatics 2024](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/40/8/btae499/7730007)
- **[DID]** The statistical-framework-for-DMS literature is the citable home for "model the counts, not the ratios" — [A statistical framework for analyzing deep mutational scanning data, PubMed 28784151](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28784151)

**Heteroscedastic / uncertainty-aware ML losses**

- **[DID]** Kendall & Gal introduce input-dependent aleatoric uncertainty, state the resulting losses *"can be interpreted as learned attenuation"*, and claim *"This makes the loss more robust to noisy data"*, with SOTA on segmentation and depth regression. (I read only the arXiv landing page; the equations were not on that page.) — [Kendall & Gal, arXiv:1703.04977](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04977)
- **[PRACTICE]** The standard form of that loss (widely reproduced; I did **not** verify the equation numbering against the full text): `L = (1/N) Σ_i [ ½·exp(−s_i)·(y_i − f(x_i))² + ½·s_i ]` where the network predicts `s_i = log σ_i²` rather than `σ_i` for numerical stability. The `exp(−s)` factor is what down-weights examples the model judges noisy.
- **[DID]** Kermut (NeurIPS 2024 spotlight) is the current reference point for *calibrated* supervised variant-effect prediction: SOTA on the ProteinGym substitution benchmark *"while providing well-calibrated uncertainties"*, but the authors are explicit that *"instance-specific uncertainty calibration remains more challenging"* and that reliability diagrams *"give the impression of good levels of calibration"* while per-instance quantification is harder — [Kermut, arXiv:2407.00002](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00002); [NeurIPS proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/34547650b2ca69d91f3b3c3ae8b21962-Paper-Conference.pdf); [code](https://github.com/petergroth/kermut)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** A related protein-property-with-uncertainties paper assumes *"a homoscedastic Gaussian noise model, on which we place a HalfCauchy prior"* — i.e. even in the uncertainty-aware corner of this field, homoscedastic noise is a common simplification — [Protein property prediction with uncertainties](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381882108_Protein_property_prediction_with_uncertainties)
- **[DID]** A dedicated benchmark of uncertainty quantification for protein engineering exists and reports RMSE/MAE/R²/Spearman — cite it when justifying *which* UQ method to pick — [Benchmarking uncertainty quantification for protein engineering, PLOS Comput Biol](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1012639)

**Censoring / dynamic range**

- **[DID]** ProteinGym names the problem directly: experiments impose *"a restrictive ceiling and/or floor"* that is *"not meaningful for protein function and mutation effect prediction"* — [ProteinGym](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.07.570727v1.full)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** On Tsuboyama's Megascale data, **20.3% of single substitutions have no ΔΔG value**, and the missing ones skew toward the most destabilising — a concrete, citable instance of informative censoring in a stability assay — [megascale-measly](https://github.com/tremgan/megascale-measly)
- **[DID]** GPS-style assays bound PSI mechanically: in the yGPS peptidome design, cells are sorted into **four gates of equal cell number (2.5 M cells each)**, and PSI is the gate-number-weighted sum of each peptide's per-gate frequency, *"from 1 … to 4"* — so PSI is a bounded, discretised quantity by construction, not an unbounded continuous one — [Conserved degronome features (bioRxiv full text)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.04.06.487275v2.full)

### Inferences

- **[REC] Ranked ladder of variance-aware options for this thesis, cheapest first.**
  1. **Train on the replicate mean, weight each example by precision.** `w_i = 1/σ̂_i²` with `σ̂_i²` from the two replicates (or, better, from a DiMSum-style pooled error model so that n=2 doesn't give you a garbage per-example variance). In PyTorch this is `loss = (w * (y-ŷ)**2).sum() / w.sum()`; in sklearn it is `sample_weight=`. Precedent: Enrich2's WLS improving replicate agreement, and DiMSum's error-weighted replicate averaging. **Cost:** ~1 day. **Failure mode:** with only 2 replicates the per-example variance is itself extremely noisy and precision weighting can *amplify* noise — this is exactly why DiMSum shares variance across variants, and why the thesis should fit a *global* variance-vs-read-depth curve rather than use the raw 2-replicate SD.
  2. **Heteroscedastic Gaussian NLL head** (`torch.nn.GaussianNLLLoss`, or write the formula above). **Cost:** ~2–3 days, one extra output channel. **Failure modes:** (a) the network can minimise loss by declaring everything uncertain — variance collapse/explosion, mitigated by predicting `log σ²` and clamping it; (b) it degrades point-prediction accuracy if the ceiling is already noise-dominated; (c) you now owe the reader a *calibration* evaluation, and Kermut shows that is hard to do convincingly.
  3. **Censored / ordinal likelihood for PSI.** Because PSI is a 4-gate weighted index with hard bounds, an ordinal (cumulative-link) or Tobit-style censored likelihood is more honest than Gaussian regression at the extremes. **Cost:** ~1 week. **Failure mode:** harder to explain to a committee; little direct precedent found in the degron literature.
  4. **Errors-in-variables / Deming regression.** Useful for the *analysis* sections (e.g. relating model scores to PSI) rather than as a training loss. `scipy.odr` implements orthogonal distance regression; statsmodels has no first-class EIV estimator. **Cost:** hours. **Failure mode:** needs a known error-variance ratio, which the audit can supply.
- **[REC] Do not pursue a full Bayesian hierarchical re-analysis of the GPS counts (a Rosace clone).** It is the statistically correct answer and it is out of scope for a few CPU-weeks and one student; cite Rosace as the principled alternative in a "limitations / future work" paragraph instead.

### Gaps

- Enrich2's and Rosace's exact formulas could not be retrieved (paywall). The thesis should read Enrich2's Methods for the random-effects variance formula (`σ²_variant = σ²_within + σ²_between`) before writing it down.
- I found **no variant-effect-prediction paper that trains with a heteroscedastic NLL on replicate-derived variances.** Kermut handles uncertainty on the *output* side (GP posterior), not by feeding per-label measurement variance into the likelihood. So "train with per-example measurement variance" appears to be a defensible but under-precedented choice in this specific field — the precedent to lean on is the MAVE *analysis* pipelines (Enrich2/DiMSum), not variant-effect predictors.

## Q3. Reframing to a more reliable target: window aggregation, within-peptide ranking, soft-set metrics, tolerance-aware scoring

### Takeaway

Window/segment aggregation is not a workaround here — it is what the closest published precedent (QCDPred on the same GPS-peptidome data type) actually does: it predicts per-peptide degron probability and **averages scores across window centres**, and the papers report **peptide-level Spearman against PSI**, never per-residue exact-match. Tolerance-aware and top-K metrics have solid precedent in the binding-site/disorder-prediction communities (Binding-site Distance Test with distance-graded partial credit; residue-Jaccard ≥ 0.75 as an "equivalent site" rule; Hit-Rate@TopK). Exact-position-set match (your 16.0%) is not a metric any of these fields uses as a headline.

### Cited Findings

**The directly relevant degron precedent**

- **[DID]** QCDPred is a **logistic regression trained on 18,599 peptides with > 50 sequencing reads** that predicts whether a **17-residue peptide** acts as a quality-control degron, using **only sequence composition**; it assigns each amino acid a value reflecting its predicted contribution to degron potency — [Prediction of Quality-control Degradation Signals in Yeast Proteins (Johansson et al., JMB)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022283622005411); [KULL-Centre code/data, via the degronome paper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.04.06.487275v2.full)
- **[DID]** Label construction in that line of work is **thresholded and ambiguity-excluded, not per-residue**: peptides with `PSI ≤ 2.2` labelled degron and `PSI ≥ 2.8` non-degron, with the band between them discarded — [yGPS-P, Biomolecules 13:987](https://www.mdpi.com/2218-273X/13/6/987) / [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/biom13060987). The degronome paper instead uses a single cutoff `PSI < 1.7` (≈ 9.5% of the peptidome) with validation groups at `PSI ≤ 1.62` — note the **thresholds differ between papers on the same assay family**, so the thesis must state which convention it follows — [degronome paper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.04.06.487275v2.full); [Nature Communications version](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35298-y)
- **[DID]** The tiling library in that screen used **17-aa (51-bp) fragments with 12-aa (36-bp) overlaps**, and the paper does **not** state how overlapping tiles were combined into per-position labels. The only explicit aggregation described is that for human peptide validation **QCDPred scores were averaged across seven amino-acid centres within each 23-aa peptide** — [degronome paper (bioRxiv full text)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.04.06.487275v2.full)
- **[DID]** Reported agreement for QCDPred is **peptide-level Spearman: ρ = −0.74** (human C-terminal peptides) and **ρ = −0.78** (yeast N-terminal peptides); experimental validation was **8/10 re-cloned peptides** matching the call, with 2 "stable"-predicted peptides showing some degron activity — [degronome paper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.04.06.487275v2.full)
- **[DID]** The same work does map degron **"centres"** along protein length (a position-level *output*), and computes degron probability for **2.2 million residues in 4,533 yeast proteins in under a minute** — so per-residue *scoring* with peptide-level *supervision and evaluation* is the established pattern — [degronome paper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.04.06.487275v2.full)

**Tolerance-aware and set-level metrics with precedent**

- **[DID]** Protein–ligand site benchmarking treats two predicted pockets as the same when *"the closest pocket centroid is within 5 Å, or overlap is at least 3/4 (≥ 75%) residues"* (residue **Jaccard ≥ 0.75**), and reports **residue-level F1 and MCC** plus pocket-level **relative residue overlap (RRO)** and relative volume overlap — [Comparative evaluation of methods for the prediction of protein–ligand binding sites, J Cheminform 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-024-00923-z); [PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11552181/)
- **[DID]** The **Binding-site Distance Test (BDT)** gives graded partial credit: *"Binding site residues predicted to be close to the observed binding site residues receive higher scores than more distant residues"*, and the same source notes MCC *"needs clearly defined observed residues, which is hard to establish for flexible proteins"* — this is the canonical citation for "±k tolerance instead of exact match" — [FunFOLDQA, PMC3364224](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3364224/)
- **[DID]** CAID2's evaluation of disordered binding regions found **AUPRC and MCC are sensitive to the fraction of positive residues** and used sampling to equalise comparisons across targets, and evaluated separately on the full dataset and on disordered regions — direct precedent for "stratify, and beware prevalence-sensitive metrics" — [Evaluation of predictions of disordered binding regions in CAID2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2001037024004318)
- **[DID]** **Hit-Rate@TopK** is in current use: *"BRHR@K, which measures whether the top-ranked model-predicted residues overlap with ground-truth binding residues"* — [arXiv:2605.24045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24045)
- **[DID]** **Top-K recall at K = top 10%** and **NDCG** are both official ProteinGym metrics, so "ranking within a unit instead of absolute calls" is benchmark-sanctioned in this exact field — [ProteinGym](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.07.570727v1.full)

### Inferences

- **[REC] Primary metric should be peptide-level, secondary metrics position-level-with-tolerance, and exact-set-match should be reported only as a diagnostic of label noise (not of the model).** Concretely, the metric suite I would defend to a committee:
  1. **Peptide level (primary):** Spearman of predicted score vs ΔPSI; AUROC/AUPRC for has-footprint. This is exactly QCDPred's reporting (ρ = −0.74 / −0.78) and exploits your 89.3% replicate agreement rather than your 0.4965 Jaccard.
  2. **Within-peptide ranking (primary for the "which residues" claim):** top-k precision and Hit-Rate@TopK with **±1 position tolerance**, plus the fraction of peptides where the argmax lands within ±1 of a replicate-positive residue. Precedents: BDT's graded distance credit, BRHR@K, ProteinGym Top-K recall.
  3. **Soft set overlap (secondary):** Jaccard / F1 computed after ±1 dilation of both sets, reported *against each replicate separately and against rep1-vs-rep2*. Precedent for a Jaccard threshold as an equivalence rule: the 0.75-residue-Jaccard rule in the ligand-site benchmark.
  4. **Window-aggregated regression (secondary):** average predictions over a 5–7-residue window before scoring, mirroring QCDPred's "averaged across seven amino-acid centres".
  5. **Exact positive-set match:** report once, next to the 16.0% replicate-vs-replicate figure, to show the metric is dominated by label noise and to justify not using it.
- **[REC]** For a saturation-mutagenesis grid, **cell-level AUROC is reasonable but prevalence-sensitive**; CAID2's finding that AUPRC/MCC move with positive rate means the thesis should report AUPRC **and** the positive rate per protein, and stratify by position (or report per-position-normalised scores) so that a few high-prevalence positions cannot carry the number.
- **[REC]** Follow the published-precedent move of **discarding the ambiguous band** (QCDPred's `PSI ≤ 2.2` / `≥ 2.8`) for the classification framing, and report how many peptides that removes. It is directly citable, it raises label reliability, and it is honest as long as you also report performance on the full unfiltered set.

### Gaps

- Neither degron paper states how overlapping tiles were turned into per-position ground truth, and neither discusses measurement noise. So **there is no published per-residue degron-footprint evaluation protocol to copy** — the thesis is defining one, which is both the opportunity and the risk.
- The degronome bioRxiv text contains an internal inconsistency (≈ 29,500 fragments synthesised vs 23,600 peptides scored) and does not report NGS replicate correlations or read-count filters; the > 50-read filter comes from the QCDPred/JMB paper instead. **[UNVERIFIED]** for both.
- I found no "soft Jaccard" metric with a formal definition in the structural-bioinformatics literature; the tolerance-aware precedents are distance-graded (BDT) or threshold-based (Jaccard ≥ 0.75), not a true soft-set metric.

## Q4. MIL under noisy instance labels: does bag-level supervision beat instance-level supervision?

### Takeaway

The honest answer for the thesis is: **the MIL literature's argument for bag/embedding-level supervision is "it avoids fitting an instance classifier you have no reliable labels for", and Ilse et al. state exactly that — but I found no controlled experiment showing bag-level supervision beating noisy instance-level supervision at a given noise rate.** Conversely, the MIL literature repeatedly reports that bag-level models are *bad at instance-level prediction*, which is a direct threat to the thesis's "which residues" claim. This cuts both ways and should be presented as an empirical question the thesis resolves by ablation, not as a settled result.

### Cited Findings

- **[DID]** Ilse, Tomczak & Welling (ICML 2018) distinguish the **instance-level** approach (classify each instance, then pool scores) from the **embedding-level** approach (embed instances, pool embeddings, then classify once). They favour the embedding route for bag-level accuracy and argue it *"avoids the extra error that comes from training an instance classifier without instance labels"*; they note the instance route's advantage is per-instance scores that identify the **"key instances"** behind a bag label. Their contribution is attention as the pooling operator, making the embedding-level model trainable and interpretable — [Attention-based Deep Multiple Instance Learning, PMLR v80](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/ilse18a/ilse18a.pdf); [arXiv:1802.04712](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.04712)
- **[DID]** The standard MIL assumption is that a bag is positive if **at least one** instance is positive and negative only if all are negative — [Instance-level Semisupervised MIL, AAAI 2008](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2008/AAAI08-102.pdf)
- **[DID]** The counter-evidence on instance localisation: *"State-of-the-art MIL models achieve competitive performance at the bag level. However, instance-level prediction, which is essential for many important applications, remains unsatisfactory."* — [Rethinking MIL for WSI classification, arXiv:2307.02249](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.02249v2)
- **[DID]** ItS2CLR derives **instance-level pseudo-labels from bag-level labels** and *"outperforms existing MIL methods in terms of both bag and instance level accuracy on three medical datasets"* — the closest thing to a method that gets both — [Liu et al., CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Liu_Multiple_Instance_Learning_via_Iterative_Self-Paced_Supervised_Contrastive_Learning_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- **[DID]** The "soft bag" line of work accepts that MIL's own labels are noisy: *"labeling noise appears on both positive and negative bags"* and *"negative bags frequently contain positive instances"*, breaking the clean-negative assumption; their soft-bag SVM uses **top instances** rather than a single max — [MIL for Soft Bags via Top Instances, CVPR 2015](http://www.svcl.ucsd.edu/publications/conference/2015/softbag/softbag_final.pdf)
- **[DID]** Pooling-operator choice matters and has been compared head-to-head for weakly-supervised *sequence* tasks (the closest structural analogue to a peptide of residues): **max vs noisy-OR** — [Comparing the Max and Noisy-Or Pooling Functions in MIL for Weakly Supervised Sequence Learning, arXiv:1804.01146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.01146)
- **[DID]** Mixed-effects MIL exists, i.e. MIL combined with random effects for clustered data — relevant because peptides from the same protein are a cluster — [Mixed Models with Multiple Instance Learning, arXiv:2311.02455](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.02455)
- **[DID]** Key-instance detection is a recognised sub-problem with dedicated methods (sparse network inversion), i.e. getting instance localisation out of a bag-trained model is an active research area rather than a free by-product — [arXiv:2009.02909](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.02909)
- **[UNVERIFIED — secondary source, no experiment]** A summary site claims MIL *"effectively mitigates noisy labeling by classifying groups of observations rather than individual points"*. Treat as a claim to verify, not evidence — [Lacuna summary](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/direction/mitigating-instance-label-noise-in-weakly-supervised-learning/txn_91caaa5846c14e19a39ab35d1c9a462b)

### Inferences

- **[REC] Frame the architecture debate as an experiment with a pre-registered prediction, which is exactly what your audit licenses.** The audit says bag-level labels (has-footprint) are 89.3% reproducible while instance-level labels (which residues) are Jaccard 0.4965. Ilse et al.'s stated rationale — embedding-level pooling avoids the error of fitting an instance classifier without reliable instance labels — maps onto your numbers almost word for word. That is a *motivation* you can cite; the *result* you must measure.
- **[REC] Run three heads on the same backbone, not two.** (1) per-position regression/classification head trained on instance labels; (2) attention-MIL (embedding-level, gated attention per Ilse et al.) trained only on the bag label, with attention weights read out as the footprint; (3) a hybrid: bag loss + a down-weighted instance loss (λ ≈ 0.1–0.3). Evaluate all three on **both** the bag metric and the tolerance-aware instance metric. Cost: the MIL head is ~50 lines on top of an existing per-position head; the whole ablation is days, not weeks, on CPU for a few-hundred-peptide set.
- **[REC] Expect, and pre-empt, the failure mode the WSI literature documents:** attention-MIL will likely win the bag metric and *lose or draw* on instance localisation. If so, the defensible thesis conclusion is "bag supervision is the right objective for the reproducible target; instance localisation needs the hybrid loss", citing [arXiv:2307.02249](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.02249v2) for why this is the expected pattern and ItS2CLR as the published remedy.
- **[REC] Use noisy-OR or top-k pooling, not max.** Max pooling puts all gradient on one residue per peptide, which with Jaccard-0.50 instance labels is close to a coin flip on which residue that is; top-k (soft bag) and noisy-OR spread it. Precedents: [soft bags / top instances](http://www.svcl.ucsd.edu/publications/conference/2015/softbag/softbag_final.pdf) and [max vs noisy-OR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.01146).

### Gaps

- **I found no paper that empirically compares bag-level vs instance-level supervision as a function of instance-label noise rate.** The theory-flavoured justification in Ilse et al. is about *absent* instance labels, not *noisy* ones. The thesis should therefore not claim "MIL is known to be more robust to instance-label noise" — that claim is, as far as I could verify, unsupported in the literature.
- No MIL paper found that is specific to residue-level protein footprints, so the architecture argument must be transferred from WSI/sound-event-detection analogues.

## Q5. Noisy-label learning generally: robust losses, co-teaching, label smoothing, consistency across replicates — and whether any of it is worth it at n ≈ a few hundred

### Takeaway

The robust-loss literature is well-developed and well-cited (MAE/GCE/SCE/APL/normalised losses) but it is almost entirely about **classification with ~10³–10⁶ training examples and symmetric synthetic label flips**. None of the sources I found validates these methods in the regime this thesis occupies (regression-ish target, n in the hundreds, structured measurement noise, not label flips). The citable facts are strong; the transfer is weak. My recommendation is to take only the cheapest two items (Huber/correlation loss, consistency across replicates) and to explicitly argue *against* co-teaching on sample-size grounds.

### Cited Findings

- **[DID]** Ghosh et al. prove MAE is noise-robust where cross-entropy is not: *"a loss based on the mean absolute value of error is inherently robust to label noise"* — [Robust Loss Functions under Label Noise for Deep Neural Networks, arXiv:1712.09482](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.09482)
- **[DID]** The cost of MAE is optimisation: *"Training with MAE has been found very challenging due to slow convergence caused by gradient saturation."* — [Normalized Loss Functions for Deep Learning with Noisy Labels, arXiv:2006.13554](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.13554) / [PMLR v119](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/ma20c/ma20c.pdf)
- **[DID]** GCE is the interpolation between CE and MAE: *"a theoretically grounded set of noise-robust loss functions that can be seen as a generalization of MAE and CCE"*; its robustness is partial — *"GCE is only robust when it reduces to the MAE loss with ρ = 1"* — [Zhang & Sabuncu, NeurIPS 2018](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/hash/f2925f97bc13ad2852a7a551802feea0-Abstract.html); [arXiv:1805.07836](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.07836)
- **[DID]** For symmetric cross-entropy, *"only its RCE term is robust"*; **Active Passive Loss (APL)** pairs two robust losses so they *"mutually boost each other"*; and the normalisation result is that *"any loss can be made robust to noisy labels by applying a simple normalization"* — [arXiv:2006.13554](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.13554)
- **[DID]** Asymmetric loss functions *"require less restrictive conditions than symmetric ones"* — [Robust Classification with Noisy Labels Based on Posterior Maximization, arXiv:2504.06805](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06805); see also [Robust Losses from Univariate Base Functions for Noisy-Label Learning, arXiv:2607.16768](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16768)
- **[DID]** The standard survey to cite for the taxonomy (robust architecture / robust regularisation / robust loss / sample selection) — [Learning from Noisy Labels with Deep Neural Networks: A Survey, arXiv:2007.08199](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.08199)
- **[DID]** Complementary-loss and loss-learning approaches exist if the thesis wants a "we considered and rejected" paragraph — [Learning from Noisy Labels with Complementary Loss Functions, AAAI 2021](https://palm.seu.edu.cn/zhangml/files/AAAI'21a.pdf); [Searching for Robustness: Loss Learning for Noisy Classification Tasks](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/thospeda/papers/gao2021arl.pdf)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Co-teaching appears in my sources only in passing: methods *"attempt to filter out noisy samples, often by co-teaching or student-teacher learning with multiple neural networks"*. I did not retrieve the Co-teaching paper itself or any result quantifying its benefit — [arXiv:2504.06805](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06805)
- **[DID — relevant precedent for the "noisy labels are structural, not flips" framing]** DiMSum's position that DMS noise is **over-dispersed count noise with a count-independent additive component**, not random label corruption — [DiMSum](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.06.25.171421v1.full)

### Inferences

- **[REC] Worth doing at n ≈ a few hundred (cheap, low-risk):**
  - **Huber loss** (`torch.nn.HuberLoss`, `delta` tuned on validation) as the main regression loss instead of MSE — it is the regression analogue of the MAE-robustness result and does not suffer MAE's full gradient-saturation problem. Cite Ghosh et al. for the principle and [arXiv:2006.13554](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.13554) for why pure MAE is a bad idea.
  - **A correlation/ranking loss** (1 − Spearman surrogate, or Pearson loss on batch) when the primary metric is Spearman. Justification is metric-alignment, and the citation is ProteinGym's choice of Spearman as the appropriate metric, not the noisy-label literature.
  - **Consistency across replicates as data augmentation, not as a regulariser**: train on both replicates as two separate examples with the same input (or on their mean), and use the replicate disagreement as the per-example weight. This is the Enrich2/DiMSum logic ported to the ML side and is far better matched to the actual noise process than symmetric-flip robust losses.
  - **Label smoothing** for the binary has-footprint head, with ε set from the measured disagreement rate: `ε ≈ (1 − 0.893) ≈ 0.107` for peptide-level labels, much larger for position-level labels. This is a principled, auditable choice of ε rather than the usual arbitrary 0.1.
- **[REC] Not worth doing at this n:** co-teaching and any sample-selection / small-loss-criterion method. These need two networks, a noise-rate schedule, and enough data that discarding the high-loss fraction still leaves a usable training set; with n in the hundreds and 76% of residual variance being noise, the "small-loss" examples are mostly the low-noise ones, so the method would systematically select the easy, high-read-depth peptides — the same selection the audit already showed does **not** reduce the noise fraction (depth 10 → 200 halved the data without helping). Say this explicitly in the thesis; it is a finding, not an omission.
- **[REC] Argue the mismatch explicitly.** The robust-loss theorems are stated for symmetric or class-conditional label flips in classification. Your noise is **heteroscedastic measurement error on a bounded continuous index**. The honest position is: "we adopt the robust-loss *principle* (bounded influence of individual residuals) via Huber, and reject the specific robust-classification losses as out-of-regime, preferring the measurement-error models from the MAVE literature (Enrich2, DiMSum) which match our noise process."

### Gaps

- No source found that evaluates robust losses at n ≤ 1000. The "is it worth it" question is answered by my reasoning above, not by a citation.
- Co-teaching's primary paper and its empirical benefits were not retrieved. If the thesis needs to cite it, fetch Han et al. 2018 directly.

## Q6. Small-n statistics: CIs and power for paired model comparisons with a few hundred correlated examples

### Takeaway

Two citable anchors cover almost everything the thesis needs: ProteinGym itself reports *"the non-parametric bootstrap standard error of the difference"* between models (so a bootstrap on the paired delta is benchmark-standard in this exact field), and Saravanan, Berman & Sober's hierarchical bootstrap is the standard reference for nested data, with the alarming headline that ignoring the nesting gives **false-positive rates above 45% at a nominal 5%**. Within-peptide residue dependence and within-protein peptide dependence make this the single highest-risk statistical issue in the thesis.

### Cited Findings

- **[DID]** ProteinGym's own model-comparison uncertainty is *"the non-parametric bootstrap standard error of the difference"* between a model's Spearman and the best model's — [ProteinGym](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.07.570727v1.full)
- **[DID]** Saravanan, Berman & Sober: neuroscience data are nested (neurons within animals within trials); *"traditional statistical tests can result in a false positive rate of over 45%, even if the Type-I error rate is set at 5%"*. The hierarchical bootstrap **resamples at each level**: first resample individuals, then resample observations within each sampled individual — [bioRxiv 819334](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/819334v2.full); [arXiv:2007.07797 (NBDT version)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.07797)
- **[DID]** The distinction the thesis must make explicit: the **cluster bootstrap** *"samples individuals with replacement and keeps all of each sampled individual's"* observations, whereas the **hierarchical bootstrap** also resamples within the cluster and is *"more conservative, reflecting random variation in both the sample of individuals and in the [observations] that they produce"* — [Saravanan et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/819334v2.full)
- **[DID]** A MATLAB reference implementation of the Saravanan method exists (useful for checking a Python port) — [Hierarchical_bootstrap_Matlab](https://github.com/jenwallace/Hierarchical_bootstrap_Matlab)
- **[DID]** A user-friendly resampling method for nested experimental designs, citing Saravanan et al. — [Analyzing nested experimental designs](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/360328019_Analyzing_nested_experimental_designs-A_user-friendly_resampling_method_to_determine_experimental_significance)
- **[DID]** The classic econometric cluster bootstrap reference — [A Bootstrap Method for Conducting Statistical Inference with Clustered Data](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/241389941_A_Bootstrap_Method_for_Conducting_Statistical_Inference_with_Clustered_Data)
- **[DID]** Paired bootstrap recipe as actually applied in benchmark papers: resample per-item scores with replacement, use the **difference in means** as the test statistic, p-value = share of resamples where the ranking reverses, **B = 10,000**, and *"per-pair p-values should be corrected for multiple comparisons"* — [arXiv:2605.24297](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24297). A variant reports the **2.5th/97.5th percentiles of the paired differences as a 95% CI** with a small-sample-corrected one-sided p-value — [arXiv:2607.24160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24160)
- **[DID]** Cluster-level resampling in practice, with the warning that matters here: *"a clustered bootstrap of 10,000 replicates"* plus a sensitivity check resampling whole families, *"since only six clusters were available"* — and the explicit caution that **with very few clusters, cluster-level inference can be unreliable** — [arXiv:2609.06330](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06330)
- **[DID]** Permutation vs bootstrap for model comparison: a paired permutation test swaps which model each observation is assigned to under an exchangeability null, and *"permutation tests are used more often than paired bootstrap tests for hypothesis testing"*; the paired bootstrap is *"similar to approximate randomization, differing mainly in sampling with replacement"* — [A Statistical Analysis of Summarization Evaluation Metrics using Resampling Methods, arXiv:2104.00054](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.00054); [arXiv:2402.06690](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.06690)
- **[DID]** Cluster-aware confidence intervals are already being reported in homology-clustered protein benchmarks — [SafeBench-Seq: a homology-clustered, CPU-only baseline … with cluster-aware confidence intervals, arXiv:2512.17527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17527)
- **[DID]** Recent methodology specifically on uncertainty in classifier performance with **nested** data — [Estimating Uncertainty in Classifier Performance with Applications to LLMs and Nested Data, arXiv:2606.26422](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26422)
- **[PRACTICE]** The attenuation literature's link to sample size: the partial-correction paper is framed around **sample size calculations** under measurement error, which is the right entry point for a power analysis when the target is unreliable — [PMC5965627](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5965627/)

### Inferences

- **[REC] The statistical plan I would write for this thesis**, in order:
  1. **Unit of resampling = protein cluster**, matching the split unit. With n ≈ 91–322 peptides the number of *clusters* may be far smaller than the number of peptides — count them and report the number. If clusters < ~20, say so and treat all p-values as indicative, citing the "few clusters → unreliable cluster inference" caution in [arXiv:2609.06330](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06330).
  2. **Two-level hierarchical bootstrap** (resample clusters with replacement, then peptides within cluster, then — if the metric is residue-level — residues within peptide), B = 10,000, per Saravanan et al. Report the **bootstrap CI of the paired delta** between models, not two separate CIs; overlapping marginal CIs is the classic misreading.
  3. **Paired permutation test** on the delta as the primary p-value (exchange model labels within peptide), with the bootstrap as the interval estimate. This matches the summarization-metrics guidance that permutation is preferred for testing while bootstrap is preferred for intervals.
  4. **Multiple-comparison control across ablations:** Holm–Bonferroni (`statsmodels.stats.multitest.multipletests(method='holm')`) if there are < ~10 ablations, Benjamini–Hochberg if more; pre-declare one primary comparison so the headline claim does not need correction. Precedent for requiring correction in paired benchmark comparisons: [arXiv:2605.24297](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24297).
  5. **Power / detectable-effect statement instead of a post-hoc power analysis.** With the bootstrap SE of the delta in hand, report the **minimum detectable difference** at 80% power ≈ 2.8 × SE(delta). Combine with the Q1 ceiling: if the attainable Spearman is ~0.30–0.37 and the bootstrap SE of a paired delta at n ≈ 100 is on the order of 0.05–0.10, then **differences smaller than ~0.15–0.25 Spearman are not detectable** — which means most architecture ablations in this thesis will be statistically indistinguishable, and the thesis should say so up front rather than over-claim a winner.
  6. **Within-peptide dependence:** never compute a residue-level CI treating residues as independent. Either aggregate to one number per peptide first (then bootstrap peptides/clusters), or use the three-level hierarchical bootstrap. The 45%-false-positive figure from Saravanan et al. is the sentence to quote when justifying this.
- **[REC] Libraries:** `scipy.stats.bootstrap` handles the paired statistic but **not** multi-level resampling — write the ~20-line nested resampler yourself and seed it (`numpy.random.default_rng(seed)`), consistent with the repo's reproducibility convention. `scipy.stats.permutation_test(..., permutation_type='samples')` gives the paired permutation test directly. `statsmodels` for Holm/BH. For the ICC in Q1, `pingouin.intraclass_corr` is the standard Python implementation.

### Gaps

- I found **no power-analysis reference specific to paired model comparison on clustered protein data.** The minimum-detectable-difference framing above is my construction from the bootstrap SE; there is no citation for it.
- No source quantifies how much the effective sample size shrinks for residue-level metrics due to within-peptide dependence; the thesis would have to estimate the design effect itself (e.g. `1 + (m̄−1)·ICC_within-peptide`).

## Q7. Pooling across datasets/batches: batch effects, per-dataset calibration, multi-task conditioning, and homology-leakage control

### Takeaway

For pooling two assays, the MAVE community's own answer is **anchor-based rescaling** (use synonymous/wild-type and nonsense/null controls to put every assay on a common scale) and **keep the raw scores separate from the normalised ones** (MaveDB's standard-score-set vs meta-analysis-score-set structure). For leakage, the field has moved past "cluster at some identity and split": the current reported pitfalls are that cluster-level splitting still leaks via shared provenance, that identity thresholds vary wildly between papers (20%/30%/50% MMseqs2, 40% CD-HIT), and that cluster-size imbalance silently shifts your actual train/test ratio.

### Cited Findings

**Cross-assay normalisation / pooling**

- **[DID]** Anchor-based normalisation in practice: a PLM fine-tuning paper normalised MaveDB datasets by *"using the mean scores of synonymous and nonsense variants as anchors"* to *"create a common scale across assays and proteins"* — [Fine-tuning Protein Language Models with Deep Mutational Scanning improves Variant Effect Prediction, arXiv:2405.06729](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.06729)
- **[DID]** MaveDB's own convention: *"Minimally transformed scores should go in as standard score sets, with meta-analysis score sets describing normalization or imputation steps"*, and a meta-analysis linked to the pre-normalisation score set *"preserves the original scores and keeps them discoverable"* — [MaveDB 2024, Genome Biology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-025-03476-y); [MaveDB v2 bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.11.29.470445v1.full)
- **[DID]** MaveDB's display scaling is *"automatically calibrated based on the scores of reference and null alleles in the dataset"* — i.e. reference/null anchoring is baked into the platform, not an ad-hoc trick — [MaveDB 2019, Genome Biology](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13059-019-1845-6)
- **[DID]** Calibration is still an unsettled engineering question in the MaveDB API, where an open issue proposes *"model a calibration's method separately from its application to a score set"* — cite this if the thesis needs to argue that cross-assay calibration lacks a settled standard — [mavedb-api issue #876](https://github.com/VariantEffect/mavedb-api/issues/876)
- **[DID]** ProteinGym's aggregation scheme is itself a batch-effect control: *"we first compute each of these metrics within groups of assays that measure similar functions"*, *"giving each functional group equal weight"*, then average the five function-type averages (Activity, Binding, Expression, Organismal Fitness, Stability) — [ProteinGym](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.07.570727v1.full)
- **[DID]** ProteinGym also **stratifies** results by MSA depth using `N_eff/L` bands (**low: < 1; medium: 1–100; high: > 100**, following Hopf et al. 2017) — a template for "report per-stratum, not just pooled" — [ProteinGym](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.07.570727v1.full) (bands reported via [secondary summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/proteingym) **[UNVERIFIED for the exact band edges]**)
- **[DID]** Supervised-split metric computation detail worth copying: Kermut computes *"Spearman and MSE … in the normalized space across folds, giving a single Spearman coefficient per assay per split rather than an average of per-fold metrics"* — this avoids per-fold small-n instability, which matters a lot at n ≈ 91 — [Kermut, arXiv:2407.00002](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00002)
- **[DID]** The two degron assays in question use **different PSI thresholds** (`PSI ≤ 2.2` / `≥ 2.8` in yGPS-P vs `PSI < 1.7` in the degronome paper) and different gate schemes — concrete evidence that pooling requires rescaling, not concatenation — [yGPS-P](https://www.mdpi.com/2218-273X/13/6/987); [degronome paper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.04.06.487275v2.full)

**Homology leakage control**

- **[DID]** Random splits inflate scores and MMseqs2 homology splitting *"is generally considered a more challenging evaluation protocol that reduces the similarity between training and test sets"* — but *"in some datasets, performance under MMseqs2 splitting is even higher than under random splitting"*, which the authors attribute to an overlooked issue — [PepBenchmark, arXiv:2604.10531](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10531)
- **[DID]** Cluster-level assignment is necessary but not sufficient: *"Row-level assignment could place the same backbone or a close homolog in both training and test through different provenance records"*, and *"the protocol does not hold out sources, databases, species, publications, or assays"* — [AMPBench-MT, arXiv:2607.25518](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25518)
- **[DID]** Threshold practice is heterogeneous: MMseqs2 at **20%, 30%, 50%** identity in different studies, CD-HIT at **40%**, and one study clustered *"using MMseqs2 with thresholds of 20% coverage and 20% sequence identity"* — so **coverage and identity must both be reported** — [AMPBench-MT, arXiv:2607.25518](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25518); [Fast-Part, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.13.623463v1.full.pdf)
- **[DID]** Random splits *"often fail to maintain sequence homology between the train and test sets, potentially overestimating the model's accuracy"* — the canonical statement of the problem, from a tool built to fix it — [Fast-Part](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.13.623463v1.full.pdf)
- **[DID]** Cluster-size imbalance shifts the realised split: *"Because cluster sizes are highly variable, this cluster-wise 80/20 split produces a sequence-wise 83/17 distribution"* — so **report the realised sequence-level counts, not the intended ratio** — [AMPBench-MT, arXiv:2607.25518](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25518)
- **[DID]** SpanSeq is a similarity-based data-splitting method built for exactly this problem in deep-learning projects — a citable alternative/complement to MMseqs2 cluster splits — [SpanSeq, arXiv:2402.14482](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.14482)
- **[DID]** Tooling for cluster-aware splitting and leakage auditing exists in Python wrappers around MMseqs2 (e.g. `protclust`) and leakage auditors, listed under the MMseqs2 GitHub topic; maintenance status unverified — [GitHub mmseqs2 topic](https://github.com/topics/mmseqs2) **[UNVERIFIED]**

### Inferences

- **[REC] Pooling plan for the two degron assays, in order of increasing cost:**
  1. **Anchor-rescale each assay independently** before pooling: map each assay's own control populations (its wild-type/stable reference and its known-degron/unstable reference, or if absent, its 5th and 95th percentiles) to a common 0–1 scale. Precedent: the synonymous/nonsense anchoring in [arXiv:2405.06729](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.06729) and MaveDB's reference/null calibration.
  2. **Keep both the raw and the rescaled table on disk**, and derive the rescaled one with a script — this is both MaveDB's convention (standard vs meta-analysis score sets) and the repo's own rule that `data/processed/` must be regenerable.
  3. **Dataset-token conditioning** (append a learned embedding for assay/compartment to every input, or add a per-dataset bias+scale output head) instead of forcing one shared scale. This is the cheapest multi-task option and costs ~10 lines. **Failure mode:** with only two datasets, the token can absorb real biology (e.g. a genuine compartment effect) and flatter the pooled metric; guard against it by reporting **per-dataset test metrics separately**, never only the pooled number.
  4. **Quantile mapping** (map assay B's score distribution onto assay A's via `scipy.stats.rankdata` + interpolation) only if the two distributions have genuinely different shapes. **Failure mode:** quantile mapping *assumes* the true effect distributions are identical across assays, which is false if the two compartments really do differ in degron prevalence — it can erase the biological signal you want. Prefer anchor rescaling, which does not make that assumption.
  5. **Always report the ProteinGym-style grouped average** (per-dataset metric, then average) rather than a pooled metric, so the larger dataset cannot dominate.
- **[REC] Leakage protocol to state explicitly in the thesis methods:** MMseqs2 `easy-cluster` with both `--min-seq-id` and `-c` (coverage) reported, whole clusters assigned to exactly one split, realised sequence-level split sizes reported alongside the intended ratio, a **random-split baseline reported next to the cluster-split result** (the gap is the leakage estimate), and an explicit note on what is *not* held out (same source paper, same assay, same protein family above the threshold) — directly following [AMPBench-MT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25518). For a tiling library, add the assay-specific pitfall that **overlapping tiles from the same protein region are near-duplicates**, so tiles must be grouped by source protein (not just by sequence identity) before splitting; otherwise a 12-aa-overlap neighbour of a test peptide sits in the training set.
- **[REC]** Compute metrics in the Kermut way — one coefficient per dataset per split over pooled fold predictions — rather than averaging per-fold Spearmans, because at n ≈ 91 per fold a per-fold Spearman is extremely unstable.

### Gaps

- I found **no paper that documents batch-effect correction specifically between two GPS/stability screens in different cell compartments.** The anchoring approach is transferred from MaveDB/PLM-fine-tuning practice, not demonstrated on this assay type.
- MaveDB does not appear to define a single canonical cross-assay normalisation; the open API issue suggests the standard is still being designed. Treat "standard practice" here as "anchor to controls and keep raw scores", not as a named procedure.
- PepBenchmark's explanation of *why* MMseqs2 splits sometimes score higher than random splits was truncated in the retrieved text; the thesis should read that section, since it bears directly on interpreting a cluster-split result that looks "too good".

## Cross-cutting recommendation (not a key question, but the report-writer should have it)

**[REC] The four highest-value, lowest-cost actions, ranked.**

1. **Switch target to the 2-replicate mean and put the replicate-vs-replicate row in every results table.** Lifts the attainable Pearson ~0.295 → ~0.374 by my arithmetic in Q1, costs nothing, and makes the ceiling argument self-evident to a committee.
2. **Move the primary metric up a level** — peptide-level Spearman/AUROC as primary (89.3% reliable), within-peptide top-k with ±1 tolerance as the "which residues" claim, exact-set-match demoted to a label-noise diagnostic. Precedent: QCDPred reports peptide-level ρ; ProteinGym uses Top-K recall and NDCG.
3. **Report cluster-level hierarchical-bootstrap CIs on paired deltas plus a minimum-detectable-difference statement**, and pre-commit to the conclusion that most ablations will be statistically indistinguishable. Quoting Saravanan et al.'s >45% false-positive figure pre-empts the obvious methods objection.
4. **Run the MIL-vs-per-position head as a clean 3-arm ablation with precision-weighted Huber loss in all arms**, and report both bag and tolerance-aware instance metrics for each arm. The literature gives a motivation (Ilse et al.) and a predicted failure mode (bag models localise poorly), but **not** an answer — so this is a genuine, publishable-in-a-thesis empirical contribution rather than a re-implementation.

**[REC] One thing to stop doing:** chasing read-depth thresholds. The audit already showed 10 → 200 halved the data without reducing the noise fraction, which is precisely what DiMSum's error model predicts — above a certain depth the residual error is the **count-independent additive term**, which no depth filter can touch ([DiMSum](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.06.25.171421v1.full)). That is a citable explanation for a negative result, and it belongs in the thesis as such.

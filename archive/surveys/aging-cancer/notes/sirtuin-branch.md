Furthermore, these sequence-centric models remain 'structurally blind', as they lack a mechanism to distinguish between high-confidence functional domains and disordered, non-informative regions. To address these limitations, we introduce SirtSAGE (Sirtuin Structural-Aware Graph-Evolutionary framework), a hybrid architecture designed to distill evolutionary embeddings through physical structural constraints. Extensive evaluations on both 5-fold cross-validation and an independent test set demonstrate that SirtSAGE outperforms state-of-the-art 1D-CNN baselines in predictive robustness, despite operating under a substantially more stringent unified multi-class setting. Our analysis reveals that SirtSAGE effectively concentrates attention on the structurally stable 'Active Core' of sirtuins while organically learning a continuous latent space whose spatial organization is qualitatively consistent with the established 4-class phylogenetic classification of mammalian sirtuins. Furthermore, successful orthogonal validation on C. elegans variants highlights the framework's mathematical rigour for cross-species homology mapping. ## Q4 — Is there a RESIDUE-LEVEL sirtuin task with a public benchmark?

### Takeaway
**Yes, and it is the strongest finding in these notes.** The 2025 *Communications Chemistry* paper (Biggar lab, Carleton University) defines exactly the task "which acetyl-lysine site is deacetylated by which SIRT isoform", and its **full training table — 6 801 sites × 443 features × 7 binary SIRT labels — is publicly downloadable from GitHub today** (I downloaded and inspected both files). Performance is genuinely low (MS-validated precision 37–43%), i.e. there is large headroom. The underlying experimental data is the Rauh et al. 2013 acetylome peptide microarray of ~6 802 human acetylation sites assayed against all seven human sirtuins.

### Cited Findings — the paper
- Ridgeway NH, Chopra A, Lukinović V, Feldman M, Charih F, Levy D, Green JR, Biggar KK. "Machine learning-driven prediction of substrates for enzymes introducing or removing protein post-translational modifications." *Communications Chemistry* 2025, 8(1):340, published 2025-11-07; PMID 41203923, PMC12594832, DOI 10.1038/s42004-025-01717-6 — **[ABSTRACT / full metadata]** According to PubMed, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-025-01717-6)
- Verbatim from the abstract: "Experimental validation confirmed that this approach correctly predicted **37-43% of proposed PTM sites**, unveiling candidate sites of the methyltransferase SET8 and the deacetylases **SIRT1-7**"; "Mass spectrometry analysis confirmed … the deacetylation of **64 unique sites identified for SIRT2**" — **[ABSTRACT]** same DOI
- Dataset, verbatim from the full text: training data was "**6801 peptides of 13 AA in length, representing the acetyl-lysine proteome**" and produced "**443 descriptive features**" — **[FULL TEXT, PMC]** [PMC12594832](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594832/)
- Feature construction: one-hot encoding + MACCS Keys (166 binary fingerprints) + ProtDCal molecular descriptors — **[FULL TEXT, PMC]** [PMC12594832](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594832/)
- Data provenance: "published data obtained from peptide microarrays", referencing **Rauh et al. (2013)**; a site was labelled "positive" if it produced "a signal ratio score of 1 or greater" — **[FULL TEXT, PMC]** [PMC12594832](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594832/)
- Models: seven separate ML-hybrid ensemble models, one per isoform SIRT1–SIRT7; each base model is **linear discriminant analysis (LDA) or a decision tree**, with SMOTE or random oversampling for imbalance; the "hybrid" part stacks the enzyme-specific base model with **MuSiteDeep's generic N6-acetyl-lysine score** — **[FULL TEXT, PMC + repo README]** [PMC12594832](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594832/); [repo README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nashirag/ML-Hybrid_Ensemble_Method/HEAD/SIRT1-7_Investigation/README.md)
- **How the test set was formed**: "stratified K-fold cross-validation" with a holdout set of "**10% of the experimental data**" for final validation; beyond that, the real evaluation is *prospective wet-lab* — predicted sites were tested by peptide array and mass spectrometry — **[FULL TEXT, PMC]** [PMC12594832](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594832/)
- SIRT2 result in detail: the ensemble "reconfirmed **64 of 149** previously confirmed SIRT2 substrates = precision **43.0%**"; the comparison baseline given is "**2.0% precision achieved by traditional permutation arrays**" — **[SNIPPET + consistent with FULL TEXT and abstract]** [Nature page](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42004-025-01717-6); [PMC12594832](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594832/)
- Non-SIRT scope: the same framework was applied to the lysine methyltransferase **SET8**, precision **37.4%** on 2 367 predicted sites — **[FULL TEXT, PMC]** [PMC12594832](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594832/)
- Stated limitations, verbatim: "abbreviated peptide sequences, which may not effectively represent full-length enzyme-substrate interactions" and the "inherently limited dataset size of known PTM sites". Stated future work: "regression-based ML models" and "**protein language models such as ESM-2 and ProtBERT**" — **[FULL TEXT, PMC]** [PMC12594832](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594832/)
- Availability statements: code at `https://github.com/nashirag/ML-Hybrid_Ensemble_Method`, archived at Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.17082245; MS data in PeptideAtlas project PASS05848; **no web server** — **[FULL TEXT, PMC]** [PMC12594832](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594832/)

## Q6 — What is genuinely unsolved here that ONE student could attack in 8–9 weeks?

### Takeaway
One candidate clears every gate: **isoform-resolved, residue-level prediction of which acetyl-lysine is deacetylated by which SIRT1–7, done with a protein language model + multi-window CNN instead of hand-crafted descriptors, evaluated under a protein-level leakage-controlled split, with XAI and a web app.** It is the paper's own stated future work, the data is verified downloadable, the dataset is tiny enough for Colab free tier, and the current ceiling (37–43% precision) is low.

### How it maps onto the advisor's five criteria (inference, not citation)
1. **Novelty**: no PLM has ever been applied to isoform-resolved SIRT substrate prediction; the only existing models are LDA/decision trees (2025). Multi-label across SIRT1–7 simultaneously (shared representation, 7 heads) has not been done at all — the 2025 work fits 7 independent models and therefore cannot exploit cross-isoform correlation. **High.**
2. **Improving a method**: descriptors → ESM-2/ProtT5 + multi-window CNN over the 13-aa window extended to full-sequence context; single multi-label msCNN head instead of 7 separate classifiers. Directly in the advisor's mCNN/msCNN family. **High.**
3. **Beyond PLM**: the honest "further than PLM" step available here is (a) adding the advisor's own newest ingredient — structural gating, since acetyl-lysines have AlphaFold models and solvent accessibility matters for whether a lysine is reachable — or (b) retrieval augmentation over the acetylome. CAUTION: structural gating is exactly SirtSAGE's move, so it must be framed as *transferring* it to a new task (residue level) rather than inventing it.
4. **XAI**: the task is unusually XAI-friendly — per-position attention/attribution over a 13-aa window directly yields an isoform-specific sequence-preference logo that can be checked against the experimentally known specificities from Rauh 2013 (e.g. SIRT6's near-exclusive preference for H3K9/H3K56-like contexts, cited **[SNIPPET]** [Sci Rep 2018 / review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7415977/)). That is a *verifiable* explanation, not a decorative SHAP plot — a stronger XAI story than BERT-Promoter's.
5. **Web app**: small model, small input (paste a sequence or a UniProt ID, get per-lysine scores for all 7 isoforms). No structure prediction needed at inference if the structure-gating variant is dropped or precomputed. Fits a FastAPI + React build in 2–3 weeks, and it would be the **only live sirtuin substrate web tool in existence** given that all three predecessors are offline.

### Risks the student must be told about (inference)
- The 10% holdout in the source paper is same-microarray; the student MUST define a stricter split (group by protein, or CD-HIT on the 13-mers, or hold out whole UniProt entries) or the thesis repeats exactly the flaw that killed the previous topic. AcetylBench 2026 is the citation that makes this a *contribution* rather than an apology.
- The labels are *in vitro peptide-array* activity, not in-cell truth. The honest framing is "predicts SIRT isoform peptide-level deacetylation activity as measured by acetylome microarray", with MS sites used as an orthogonal check. Over-claiming "predicts in-vivo substrates" is the obvious reviewer attack.
- Severe class imbalance per isoform (the 2025 paper needed SMOTE/oversampling at >20:80 imbalance) — report MCC/AUPRC, not accuracy.
- 2026 competition is real but adjacent, not head-on: SirtSAGE (sirtuin classification, the advisor's own), PLMGraph-Ace (generic species-specific acetylation sites, PLM+graph), AcetylBench (benchmarking), Sci Rep SIRT2 inhibitor screening. None of them does isoform-resolved SIRT substrate prediction. Flag all four to the advisor so he sees the student has checked.

Introduction · 1 of 5

Proteins are chains of amino acid residues

Proteins do the work of the cell — catalysis, signalling, structure, transport. A protein is a linear chain of amino acids; each link is called a residue.

  • 20 standard amino acid types
  • The order of residues is the sequence: MKTAYIAKQR…
  • Sequence determines the 3-D fold; the fold determines function
Two words used throughout this talk
Residueone position in the chain — “position 7”
Amino acid typewhat sits at that position — “valine”

A mutation changes the amino acid type at one residue: M7V = methionine → valine at position 7.

own diagram. Residue = a position; amino acid type = what occupies it.

Introduction · 2 of 5

Protein level is set by a balance: synthesis against degradation

A protein's concentration in the cell is not set by production alone.

amount in cell = rate of synthesis − rate of degradation

Cells destroy proteins on purpose, and constantly:

  • to switch signals off on schedule — cell-cycle regulators, transcription factors
  • to clear misfolded or damaged copies before they aggregate
  • to adjust quickly to changed conditions
Consequence. A protein that is made correctly but destroyed too fast is just as absent as one that was never made. Degradation is a control layer in its own right.

Introduction · 4 of 5

A degron is the recognition signal — and the question is which residues carry it

A degron is the short segment of a protein that an E3 ligase recognises in order to mark it for degradation. It is the “address label” of step 1.

  • Typically a short linear motif, a few to a few tens of residues
  • Can sit at the N-terminus, the C-terminus, or internally
  • Degron strength is graded, not binary: strong → intermediate → inactive
The question this thesis asks is one level finer

Within a degron, which individual residues determine its strength — and by how much does the strength change when one of them is substituted?

Scope statement

Labels here are measured on isolated 28-residue peptides fused to a reporter, so the assay sees autonomous degrons: signals that work inside the 28-mer on their own. Degrons that need priming — for example phosphorylation by another kinase — are outside its reach. Predictions describe peptide-level stability, and do not by themselves establish an effect on the intact protein or any therapeutic effect.

own diagram. Zoom: whole protein → the 28-mer the assay measures → the residues that matter.
“Autonomous degrons” is a measured property of the assay, not an assumption — see slide 6.

Related work · 1 of 5 · the precondition for reading the next table

How degron data is measured: the GPS stability assay

Every dataset on the next slide comes from one experimental idea — Global Protein Stability (GPS) profiling, introduced by Koren et al., Cell 2018.

StepWhat is done
1Synthesise a library of short peptides, each fused to a fluorescent reporter (GFP)
2Express one peptide per cell, alongside a second, stable reporter as internal control
3Sort cells by the reporter ratio using FACS, into stability bins
4Sequence each bin; a peptide's bin distribution gives its stability score
The resulting label — Protein Stability Index (PSI)
1 — low PSI
degraded quickly = strong degron 6 — high PSI
stable = not a degron

Scale 1 … 6 (6 bins) in the dataset used here. Bin counts differ between papers — Zhang 6 bins, Voutsinos 4, Larsen 4 gates on a reversed scale.

Mutation effect = a difference between two measured constructs
ΔPSI = PSI(mutant) − PSI(wild type)

Keep the sign straight. This is the one convention that has been got wrong before in this project: low PSI = degraded, and ΔPSI > 0 = the mutation rescued the peptide. Every figure in this talk keeps the same direction.

Koren I, Timms RT, Kula T, Xu Q, Li MZ, Elledge SJ. Cell 173(7):1622–1635.e14, 2018. DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2018.04.028

Gap · 1 of 3 · the strongest asset of this thesis

The gap is provable, not merely unexplored

The closest published model for degron strength, PAP, cannot answer the residue-level question — and this is a mathematical property of the model class, not a shortfall in training.

The argument
  • PAP's internal channel is Conv1D(kernel=1) → GlobalAveragePooling, so its score is a function of amino acid composition only
  • For such a model, the predicted effect of substituting a → b is Δf(a→b) = f(c − ea + eb) − f(c) — the right-hand side contains no position index
  • Therefore, for any group of substitutions sharing the same amino acid pair, the model must assign identical effects, whatever the positions
How often that breaks the task — own measurement, scanning mutagenesis set
Peptides with a measured footprint2,624
…of which have a same-substitution-pair group containing both a positive and a non-positive position2,353 — 89.7%

On those peptides a composition-only model is provably unable to separate the residues that matter from the ones that do not.

And the gap now has a price, not only a proof
+0.0289
cell-level AUROC from adding local-neighbourhood features to an otherwise composition-only model
95% CI [0.0264, 0.0313] · 1,599 protein clusters · 2,288 peptides
own diagram, own measurement — scripts and JSON result files in the repository.
PAP is not a bad model — it was trained for a segment-level score. Using it for residue-level effects is using it outside its design.

Problem definition · 1 of 3

Input, output, label

Task

Given a degron peptide, predict the effect of every possible single substitution on its stability, at residue resolution.

Inputa 28-residue peptide sequence (optionally its structural context: rASA, pLDDT)
Output 1a 28 × 20 matrix of predicted ΔPSI — one value per (position, amino acid type)
Output 2a per-position importance map — the 19 substitutions at each position aggregated
LabelΔPSI = PSI(substituted) − PSI(wild type), both measured in the GPS assay
Label signΔPSI > 0 ⇒ the substitution stabilised the peptide ⇒ that residue was part of a working degron
Unit of inferencethe protein cluster (MMseqs2, ≥30% identity, 80% coverage) — not the peptide, and not the residue
Two framings of the same output, both reported
  • cell-level — is this one (position, amino acid) substitution stabilising? → AUROC primary metric
  • position-level — which positions carry the degron? → precision@3 secondary
Scope

Peptides are measured in isolation, fused to a reporter, in the cytosol. Results describe that system. Transfer to intact proteins is a separate question with its own labels.

own diagram. The matrix panel is schematic; slide 16 shows a real measured one.
Cluster-level, not peptide-level, is the unit of inference — ignoring the nesting inflates false positives.

"Unit of inference is the protein cluster" phải nói sớm. Nhưng sau phiên 8 thì mức phạt đã đo được và nhỏ hơn lo ngại: thư viện bão hòa chọn mỗi protein một peptide ⇒ 91 peptide trên 91 protein / 89 cụm, lồng nhau ở mức protein gần như không có.

Phạt thật nằm ở ô lồng trong peptide: ICC 0,0625, cỡ cụm trung bình 526 ⇒ hệ số thiết kế 33,79, tức ≈ 1.416 ô độc lập hiệu dụng — không phải 47.847, và cũng không phải 91.

Con số "dương giả trên 45%" là cảnh báo chung từ y văn, không phải phép đo trên dữ liệu này — đừng trích như số của đề tài.

⚠ Thước đo chính của ma trận là AUROC mức ô (trần 0,9519), không phải Spearman (trần chỉ 0,6989). Đừng đảo lại.

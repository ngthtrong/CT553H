\section{Problem Statement}
The abundance of a protein in a cell depends on both its synthesis and its degradation.
In the ubiquitin--proteasome system, a \emph{degron} is a short sequence element that marks a protein for
selective degradation \citep{varshavsky2019,koren2018}. A single amino acid substitution inside a degron can
strengthen or weaken this signal, so knowing the effect of each substitution helps researchers choose
mutations to test experimentally \citep{zhang2023,voutsinos2026}.

Existing experiments score a whole peptide with one activity value. That value does not say which
position, or which substituted amino acid, makes the degron signal stronger or weaker. Measuring every
substitution experimentally is costly, which motivates a predictive model that shortlists substitutions.
Such a model is only useful if it is evaluated on experimental labels and if similarity between training
and test proteins is controlled.

\section{History of Approaches to the Problem}
\subsection{International Context}
Large-scale stability profiling based on the Global Protein Stability (GPS) assay \citep{yen2008} has produced
experimental degron maps, including scanning and saturation mutagenesis of human peptides
\citep{zhang2023}, cytosolic tile maps \citep{voutsinos2026} and transcription-factor tile maps
\citep{larsen2025}. On the
computational side, PAP and Degformer predict peptide-level degron activity or protein stability
\citep{voutsinos2026,degformer}, MetaDegron predicts E3-ligase-targeted degron motifs \citep{zheng2024},
and DEGRONOPEDIA and DegronMD provide annotation resources \citep{degronopedia,degronmd}.

These works address peptide- or motif-level prediction and annotation. Residue-level substitution
effects measured on a common assay endpoint, evaluated with protein-cluster-aware splits, remain
a question that the thesis examines rather than a gap it assumes is unfilled.

\section{Objectives}
\subsection{General Objective}
To study methods that predict the effect of amino acid substitutions in degrons from experimental data,
to evaluate generalisation across protein clusters, and to build a tool that supports analysis and
prioritisation of mutations.

\subsection{Specific Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Build a reproducible pipeline that normalises data, maps coordinates and derives mutation-effect
        labels while keeping each source and assay condition separate.
  \item Establish baselines: amino acid composition, ridge regression, the PAP score difference and the
        Degformer architecture, under clearly stated comparable conditions.
  \item Test whether representations learned from peptide-level labels and fine-tuned on mutation labels
        improve prediction, and study the learning curve before choosing a model.
  \item Evaluate effectiveness, stability across seeds, error, and the contribution of each component.
  \item Build a software prototype that accepts a peptide, shows the predicted effect matrix, ranks
        substitutions and exports results with model information.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Subject and Scope}
\subsection{Research Subject}
Degron peptides, single amino acid substitutions, protein stability indices measured with a reporter
assay, and models predicting residue-level mutation effects. A \emph{residue} is one amino acid at a
specific position; the \emph{footprint} is the map of positions or substitutions that change degron
activity under a given assay.

\subsection{Research Scope}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The main label source is the human peptide data of Zhang et al.\ \citep{zhang2023}; other peptide-level
        sources are used only for transfer learning or domain studies when their terms of use allow.
  \item Input: reference (wild-type) sequence, position and substituted amino acid. Output: predicted
        effect $\Delta$PSI, which can be arranged into an $L\times 20$ matrix.
  \item Identifying the specific E3 ligase is not a main task. No new wet-lab experiments are performed,
        and software output supports hypothesis generation; it does not establish causation, disease
        effects or therapeutic benefit.
\end{itemize}
